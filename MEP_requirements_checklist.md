# MEP Requirements Checklist

Version: 1.0
Prepared for: Combination Automotive & Diesel Mechanics Training Facility
Prepared by: Project Team
Date: 2026-03-09

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document provides the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing (MEP) requirements for the 24‑student Automotive & Diesel Mechanics Training Facility. It is a formal requirements checklist intended for use by architects, MEP engineers, and facilities staff to:

- Define expected loads, equipment, and zones
- Set constraints for routing and coordination
- Guide schematic and construction documentation
- Establish assumptions and future‑proofing requirements

This document is not a construction drawing. All design work must comply with applicable local codes and standards.

## 2. Project Summary and Assumptions

- Occupancy: 24 students plus instructors and support staff (specify shifts if multi‑shift use is planned).
- Typical bay dimensions and counts (nominal; confirm in architectural package):
  - Heavy‑Duty Diesel Bays: 2 bays (large/heavy vehicles)
  - Light‑Duty Automotive Bays: 6 bays
  - EV/Hybrid Bay(s): 1 bay (dedicated EV charging & battery work)
  - Alignment / ADAS Bay: 1 bay
  - Inspection / Rapid Service Bays: 2 bays
  - Electronics / Diagnostic Lab (clean room): 1 room
  - Support spaces: admin, classrooms, storage, mechanical, electrical rooms
- Fixed equipment will be installed per the Equipment Schedule (to be provided). Where exact specs are unknown, provide conservative design loads (see Section 5).
- Overhead‑routing is the preferred strategy for power, compressed air, and fluids unless explicitly noted.

## 3. Deliverables for MEP Engineers

Prior to detailed design, deliver the following to the design team:

- Functional Narrative: use cases, occupancy patterns, and equipment lists by bay.
- Bay‑by‑bay Equipment Schedule (spreadsheet) with provisional electrical and mechanical loads.
- Architectural floor plan with fixed equipment and bay identifiers.
- Site constraints and existing utility locations.

## 4. Bay‑by‑Bay Requirements (high level)

Each bay entry below should be expanded in the Equipment Schedule with specific models, motor sizes, and actual loads.

- Heavy‑Duty Diesel Bays (per bay):
  - Dedicated exhaust extraction rails with flexible capture; high‑CFM per bay (design for source capture plus room ventilation).
  - 480V service available at mechanical equipment; 240V/120V at tool benches.
  - Floor drains/trench drains sized for heavy duty wash and oil retention.
  - Provision for lifts (specify lift models) and associated power and structural requirements.

- Light‑Duty Automotive Bays (per bay):
  - Source‑capture for welding and localized exhaust; portable extraction arms recommended.
  - 240V circuits for hoists or specialty equipment; 120V for general tools.
  - Overhead power drops preferred; floor boxes only where required.

- EV/Hybrid Bay:
  - High‑amp 240V/480V circuits for chargers; dedicated EV charger space with future expansion capacity.
  - Battery storage / quarantine zone provisions (ventilation, containment, suppression per NFPA guidance).
  - Sensitive electronics grounding and isolated HVAC zone.

- Alignment / ADAS Bay:
  - Large open floor plan with minimal overhead obstructions; precise floor flatness requirements.
  - Power for alignment equipment and data connections at fixed bench locations.

- Electronics / Diagnostic Lab:
  - Stable temperature control and filtration (higher MERV rating) to limit dust.
  - Isolated power circuits and surge protection for diagnostic equipment.

## 5. Electrical Requirements

- Design approach: provide provisional loads for schematic design; final loads to be supplied when equipment schedule is complete.
- Panel and service sizing:
  - Provide main service sized with 20–30% spare capacity above provisional loads.
  - Include dedicated subpanels for heavy equipment and EV charging clusters.
- Voltage and circuits:
  - 120/208V or 277/480V three‑phase depending on site standard — confirm with owner. Specify locations requiring 480V.
  - Identify and locate dedicated 240V single‑phase and 480V three‑phase circuits for lifts, dynos, and large compressors.
  - Provide distributed 120V GFCI‑protected outlets around bays and work benches.
- Distribution and convenience power:
  - Overhead power drops are preferred for flexibility; provide layout for rails and drops.
  - Provide flush floor boxes where equipment requires it (maintenance access and isolation considered).
- EV charging:
  - Reserve electrical capacity and space for expansion of EV chargers; provide conduit raceways to charger zones sized for future feeders.
- Grounding and bonding:
  - Ensure equipotential bonding in labs and charger zones; provide dedicated grounding points for diagnostic equipment.

## 6. HVAC & Ventilation Requirements

- Zoning:
  - Separate zones: Shop (bays), Electronics Lab, Admin/Classrooms, Storage, Battery Quarantine.
  - Controls should allow independent setback/occupancy modes.
- Diesel exhaust capture:
  - Design high‑capacity source capture rails above diesel bays; specify capture efficiency and CFM targets (to be confirmed by mechanical engineer and code requirements).
  - Include make‑up air provisions sized to balance exhaust and maintain a controlled environment.
- Source capture for light duty:
  - Provide capture arms or overhead rails where welding, soldering, or localized contamination occurs.
- Filtration and IAQ:
  - Provide filtration levels appropriate to each zone (e.g., MERV 8–13 for shop supply, higher for electronics lab).
  - Include CO/NOx monitoring in enclosed diesel areas and alarms tied to ventilation control.
- Temperature control:
  - Electronics Lab: tight tolerance (±2–3°C). Shop areas: wider tolerance acceptable.
- Battery storage ventilation:
  - Design ventilation, monitoring, and isolation for battery storage per manufacturer and NFPA guidance.

## 7. Plumbing, Waste, and Compressed Air

- Drainage:
  - Provide trench drains in designated service bays and concrete bays; slope and sizing per code and wash use.
  - Floor drains must route to appropriate oil/water separator or segregated waste system.
- Waste oil and fluids:
  - Provide waste oil tank(s) with pump‑down capabilities and containment; define fill access.
  - Plan for DEF and coolant refill points with appropriate piping and containment.
- Compressed air:
  - Central compressor(s) with ring main piping; provide drops with shutoffs and regulators at each bay.
  - Install condensate traps and automatic drains; provide maintenance access.
- Water and sanitary fixtures:
  - Adequate handwashing and restrooms sized for occupancy; eyewash and safety showers located near chemical handling.

## 8. Fire Protection, Life Safety, and Environmental Controls

- Fire suppression:
  - Coordinate suppression strategy with Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ); vehicle bays may require specialized suppression.
- Detection and monitoring:
  - Install CO monitoring in diesel areas and integrate with building alarm system.
  - Smoke detectors where appropriate; consider heat detectors in shop environments where smoke detectors may nuisance alarm.
- Emergency systems:
  - Clearly identified emergency shutoffs for electrical and fuel supplies.
  - Identify and locate spill containment and cleanup equipment.

## 9. Mechanical Rooms and Service Spaces

- Mechanical room sizing:
  - Provide space with clearance for HVAC, compressors, pumps, and controls with future expansion space.
- Access and maintenance:
  - Route large equipment to be serviceable without dismantling critical structure; provide crane or service access if required.

## 10. Routing, Coordination, and Penetrations

- Overhead service corridors:
  - Provide coordinated overhead routing corridors for power, air, and fluids where possible.
- Slab penetrations & trench coordination:
  - Identify locations for major slab penetrations and trenches during schematic phase.
- Coordination drawings:
  - Produce MEP coordination drawings (3D or combined sheet) prior to construction documents to avoid clashes.

## 11. Controls, Monitoring, and Commissioning

- Building Management System (BMS):
  - Points for HVAC, ventilation, CO monitoring, and major equipment to be included.
- Energy monitoring:
  - Provide meters for main service and major sub‑feeds (EV charging, compressors, major HVAC mechanicals).
- Commissioning:
  - Include functional testing of extraction systems, HVAC balancing, and control sequence verification.

## 12. Future‑Proofing and Spare Capacity

- Reserve 20–30% spare feed capacity in main electrical service for future expansion.
- Provide spare conduits and feeder raceways to EV/charger zones and mechanical rooms.
- Use modular overhead systems for rails and extraction to ease future reconfiguration.

## 13. Regulatory and Standards References

- Applicable codes and standards (confirm local AHJ):
  - National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) standards (as applicable)
  - International Building Code (IBC)
  - Local electrical and plumbing codes
  - Manufacturer guidance for extraction, battery systems, and chargers
